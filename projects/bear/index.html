01

Project scope

BEAR is a six-person capstone project developing a twin-fuselage UAV for local search, thermal imaging, and medical-kit delivery. The aircraft combines vertical takeoff and hover with a fixed-wing configuration.

I owned most mechanical design, including the airframe and aerostructures, payload, and hardware packaging.

02

Airframe architecture

The twin fuselages package batteries, powertrain hardware, and tilt mechanisms locally. I developed the carbon-fiber plate-and-tube frame, modular wing interfaces, and printed tail connectors around that configuration.

Replaceable wing sections use telescoping spars and bonded root plates with threaded interfaces. Bonded end caps and keyed connectors secure the tail structure without drilling through the tubes. I also completed beam-bending calculations to support spar sizing.

03

Deployable payload

I developed the detailed payload design from an initial team concept: an inner medical-kit box retained within an outer casing, with a servo-actuated slider releasing the payload. The assembly reserves space above the box for a packed parachute.

Printed prototypes exposed latch compliance and clearance issues. I revised the clip lid, added locating features, tuned tolerances, and used a CAD motion study to check the mechanism through its travel.

04

Avionics and battery packaging

The center-wing avionics bay houses the flight controller, GPS, telemetry, and receiver, with optical-flow and thermal sensors below. I arranged mounting and access within the wing profile while separating the radio hardware.

The battery mount went through three designs: the first had clearance problems, the second was difficult to use, and the third combined accessible retention with a protective structure. Avionics-bay prototypes also revealed cracking around openings and print warping; local reinforcement and adjusted spar-hole positions restored the fit.

05

Electrical integration

I routed powertrain and actuator harnesses through the airframe to the avionics bay. Powering actuators locally from ESC BECs reduced the central wire routing from 36 conductors to 12 signal wires.

06

Field testing

The team progressed from bench integration to hover and outdoor flight trials. The gallery includes payload tests, loiter and return-to-launch trials, demo rehearsals, and failure footage.
